# Evidence-Based Study & Learning Strategies

## 1. Active Recall vs. Passive Review
- **The Principle**: Testing yourself forces your brain to retrieve information from long-term memory, strengthening neural connections. Passive review (re-reading, highlighting) creates an illusion of competence without deep encoding.
- **Actionable Steps**:
  1. Close your notes after reading a section.
  2. Write down or explain aloud everything you can remember without looking.
  3. Reopen notes to identify omissions and correct misunderstandings immediately.
  4. Use flashcards (Anki, Quizlet) or practice exam questions instead of re-reading summaries.

## 2. Spaced Repetition
- **The Forgetting Curve**: Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered that memories decay exponentially unless refreshed at spaced intervals.
- **Interval Schedule**:
  - Review 1: 1 day after learning.
  - Review 2: 3 days later.
  - Review 3: 7 days later.
  - Review 4: 16 to 30 days later.
- **Leitner Box System**: Move flashcards to higher-numbered boxes with longer intervals as you get them right; reset to Box 1 when you fail a card.

## 3. The Feynman Technique for Conceptual Mastery
- **Step 1: Choose a concept**: Write the concept title at the top of a blank page.
- **Step 2: Teach it to a 12-year-old**: Explain the concept using simple, jargon-free everyday language and analogies.
- **Step 3: Identify knowledge gaps**: When you get stuck or find yourself using complex jargon as a crutch, return to source material to relearn.
- **Step 4: Refine and simplify**: Polish your explanation until it flows smoothly and makes intuitive sense.

## 4. The Pomodoro Technique & Deep Work
- **Classic Rhythm**: 25 minutes of uninterrupted focus on a single task, followed by a 5-minute restorative break (no screens; stretch, walk, hydrate).
- **Extended Rhythm (Ultradian Cycles)**: 50 minutes of deep focus followed by 10-15 minutes of recovery.
- **Rules**: If an off-topic thought strikes during a cycle, write it down on a "Distraction Sheet" and immediately return to your task.
